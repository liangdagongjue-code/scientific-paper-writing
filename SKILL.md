# Scientific Paper Writing

## Purpose

Apply the author's reusable writing preferences across papers and disciplines: a focused research question, progressive reasoning, explicit mechanisms, clear evidence, and concise, supported statements of value. Transfer rhetorical functions, not another project's methods, data, section numbering, or editorial decisions.

These preferences were generalized on 2026-10-11. They are a writing framework, not a claim that one format fits every discipline or guarantees acceptance.

## Choose the relevant guidance

- For an outline, new draft, section rewrite, or structural review, read the complete [argument and section guide](references/argument-and-sections.md).
- For prose editing, citation placement, translation, or final consistency review, read the complete [language and evidence guide](references/language-and-evidence.md).
- For a full manuscript rewrite, read both guides.
- The [中文通用写作指南](references/中文通用写作指南.md) explains the same approach for the author. English guides are the operational instructions; do not create conflicting rules between languages.

This skill is self-contained. It requires no particular paper, project, bibliography, dataset, simulator, publisher, or local manuscript directory.

## Start with the requested mode

1. Identify the genre, audience, venue constraints if known, available evidence, and requested output: analysis, outline, review draft, translation, revision, or file integration. Use supplied context instead of asking the author to repeat it.
2. State the central question and supported answer internally. For substantial drafting, identify the problem's importance, what prior work establishes, the specific unresolved issue, the insight, and the evidence supporting each proposed contribution.
3. When necessary information is missing, ask a focused question or use clearly identified draft placeholders. Do not invent literature gaps, measurements, theoretical results, participant details, or publication status.
4. For a substantial new draft, the author's default is Chinese conceptual review followed by complete, natural English translation. Honor direct requests for English or another workflow. Literal translation of an existing source must preserve the source rather than apply a rewrite.
5. Edit manuscript files only when requested. Preserve unrelated content, data, code, and existing author decisions. Do not equate a proposed draft with integrated or submitted text.

## Reusable principles

1. **One argumentative center:** make the problem, gap, contribution, and evidence form a connected chain. Each section should advance that chain at a different resolution.
2. **Progressive explanation:** explain why a design or analytical step is needed, what it does, and what capability or inference it enables. Avoid component inventories without reasoning.
3. **Contribution-led tone:** foreground supported advances and their importance. Avoid habitual defensive endings after every positive claim, but retain information needed to interpret the evidence.
4. **Concision without logical loss:** remove duplicated functions, boilerplate, and process chatter. Preserve causal bridges, informative comparisons, and approved substantive coverage.
5. **Correct evidence ownership:** cite borrowed premises and prior methods; support original findings with the paper's own evidence. Citation density is not a substitute for relevance.
6. **Proportionate language:** strong evidence warrants strong wording. Uncertainty words are legitimate when warranted; novelty, universality, causality, and statistical significance require their own support.
7. **Functional, not rigid structure:** adapt section names, counts, and ordering to the genre and venue. Do not require experiments in a theoretical paper or a separate Related Work chapter in every field.
8. **Real transitions:** connect paragraphs by the question or concept passed between them. A sequence of “however” and “moreover” does not create an argument.
9. **Faithful translation and revision:** natural English may change syntax, not the approved claim, metric, assumptions, citation ownership, or substantive coverage. Do not silently shorten an accepted full draft.
10. **Project independence:** do not import another paper's task counts, algorithms, numerical examples, preferred fonts, fixed six-section layout, omissions of timing/disclosures, or submission status.

## Default author preferences, adaptable to the task

- Abstract: a compact research story, normally one paragraph; gap → approach → enabled advantage → overall finding → implication. Keep numbers only when central to the takeaway or required by the genre/venue.
- Introduction: documented real problem → unresolved tension → core question → prior capabilities and gap → insight → proposed contribution and evidence. Prefer one coherent closing contribution/evidence paragraph rather than repeated result summaries.
- Related work: synthesize other researchers' contributions; position the current paper briefly near the end instead of advertising it in every paragraph. Explicit local comparisons remain appropriate when necessary.
- Results: question → comparison or analysis → principal finding → selective supporting evidence → interpretation. Let tables carry exhaustive values.
- Conclusion: synthesize what the study establishes and why it matters; derive future questions from real assumptions or findings. Prefer a compact single paragraph when adequate, not at the expense of required content.
- Prefer verified published primary scholarship when it supports the claim; retain technically necessary foundational work. Unpublished work remains relevant to novelty checking, and its actual status must never be misrepresented.

These are defaults, not bans on structured abstracts, numerical summaries, several conclusion paragraphs, unpublished sources, or different section layouts. The current author request, scientific question, and applicable venue requirements determine exceptions.

## Completion check

Can a reader identify the problem, exact gap, new contribution, supporting evidence, and warranted implication without reconstructing missing steps? Are citations, notation, figures, quantitative claims, and main/supplement scope consistent? Is the prose clear and assertive without claiming more than the study establishes?

Report what was actually delivered and any unresolved evidence gaps. Following this skill alone does not authorize new experiments, remote access, submission, or changes to disclosure requirements.
